## 步骤

### 第 1 步：Battery Tester for Cleaning Robots

A simple and easy-to-make battery status tester for cleaning robots like Conga, Roomba, Cecotec, and other similar 14.4V models. You will need some wires, a 12V P21W automotive turn signal bulb (similar bulbs like the PY21W amber also work; a 21W bulb is recommended), a push-button, and an electrical terminal block.
You will also need some needle-type test probe terminals to pierce the small connector; you can substitute these with needles soldered to the test wire or needles held in place with a terminal block—it's just a matter of improvising with what you have at home.
Photo 2: I show the very simple diagram to follow. In my case, I soldered one of the push-button terminals directly to the bulb base. Depending on the model of push-button you have (I salvaged this one), you can also mount it separately using a piece of wire.

### 第 3 步

Example of how to use the tester, ONLY ACTIVATING THE BULB using the button; if the bulb no longer lights up with this test, we could already consider the dead battery as discarded and faulty. If we have another battery to compare and the bulb lights up with that one,
We could also connect the multimeter as an additional check to confirm that the dead battery is actually below 6 V or is at 0V, just in case the lamp is burnt out.
Personally, if I am certain that the lamp is OK and the dead battery no longer passes this test, I consider it faulty.

### 第 8 步

Photo 1: With a multimeter added to the cable + test lamp, we can measure the battery's voltage at open circuit, without any load or consumption connected. Note: This voltage is ALWAYS higher than the actual voltage the battery has.
Photo 2: With the button activated, the light bulb is connected, which applies a load to the battery. The voltage in a functioning battery can drop between 1 and 2 V MAXIMUM; if it drops more or goes almost to 0, the battery is in poor condition and no longer holds a charge.
Photo 2: If the light bulb does not turn on when applying the load to the battery, it is clearly a DEAD BATTERY. It must be replaced as it no longer holds a charge and CANNOT support the power draw of a 21 W bulb. On the voltmeter, we will see it show a few volts, less than 6 V, or even almost 000 V.
